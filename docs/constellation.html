The coin
A SWAP test. Its ancilla reads 0 with probability P(0) = (1 + F)/2, where F is the overlap of the two registers: identical states always give 0, orthogonal ones give 0 half the time. "How alike" above is F.
Match rule
Lock when the one-sided 95% lower bound on P(0) clears 0.90 (p̂ − 1.645σ ≥ 0.90), as in run_blind_rus_pose_recovery. With perfect flips, P(0) ≥ 0.95.
Why the peak misses by a few degrees
The circuit applies Utube(τ) to your copy's register only. That nudges the peak a few degrees off the exact answer and caps P(0) just under 1. At τ = 0 the peak lands on the exact angle with P(0) = 1.
What is being matched
Pose recovery: the ligand against a copy of itself turned about the z axis through its centroid. Not docking against the pocket, which is read from the same entry for context only.

How it works

The 30-second version

No quantum background needed. The exact method is at the bottom.

  1. Medicines are keys

    A drug works by fitting into a pocket on a protein, like a key in a lock, and a key only works when it is turned the right way. Here we take real drugs out of real crystal structures, turn them out of place, and ask whether a quantum computer can turn them back.

  2. The catch: it is blindfolded

    The quantum computer never sees where the molecule belongs. It cannot look at the gold ghost. It can only ask one question: how alike are these two shapes?

  3. The quantum coin

    The true pose goes into one set of qubits and your guess into another. A short circuit called a SWAP test then sets one qubit that acts like a coin: identical shapes always land heads, shapes with nothing in common land 50/50. Flip it a few hundred times and count: more heads means a closer match.

  4. Warmer, colder

    The blind search turns the molecule by a random amount, flips the coin again, and keeps the turn only if it got more heads. It stops when it is 95% sure that at least 9 flips in 10 land heads. That gets it close, not exact: within about 20° of the answer.

For quantum folks: the exact method
Task
Pose recovery, not docking. The ligand is matched against a copy of itself turned about the z axis through its centroid. The pocket (heavy atoms within 5 Å) is read from the same entry for context only: it is a different molecule, so no angle would make the two registers agree.
Encoding
Atoms are grouped into radial shells, one qubit per shell, balanced so no qubit is left empty, with atoms within about 0.25 Å of a boundary shared between the two shells so coordinate error cannot flip an atom between qubits. Each phase is arg(Mk)/k of the shell's weighted angular moment Mk = Σ wi eikφi, with w = √(Z/6)·e1.5 ẑ and k the lowest order that does not cancel. A rotation shifts every phase by the same angle, the register ignores atom order, and it tells mirror images apart.
Circuit
4 shells: 9 qubits (4 target, 4 probe, 1 ancilla), 62 PhasedX and 32 ZZPhase after rebasing to the H-series gate set. 8 shells: 17 qubits, 122 PhasedX, 64 ZZPhase.
Cost
HQC = 5 + (N1q + 10 N2q + 5 Nm)·shots/5000 for each circuit the search runs: 13.64 HQC per 100-shot circuit at 9 qubits, 22.04 at 17.
Result
The blind search locks on all six, in 1–5 steps at 9 qubits and 1–8 at 17, landing 0.4–20° and 0.4–15° from the exact angle (benchmarks/molecular_showdown.json). A lock means the pose is inside the band where P(0) clears the bar: at 500 shots, up to about 25° from the exact angle with 4 shells and 20° with 8 (hydrogen, which barely changes under a turn, about 45°). The larger register is no faster, but it tightens that band and makes different molecules harder to confuse (worst false match 0.71 → 0.56).
