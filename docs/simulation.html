How 3D Molecular Coordinates Become Quantum Phases: Let a molecule contain N atoms at Cartesian positions (x_i, y_i, z_i) with partial charges q_i. The classical HPC bridge computes the geometric center of mass (COM) and groups atoms into 4 angular quadrature sectors around the principal rotational axis:
φ_m = atan2(Δy_m, Δx_m) × sgn(q_m),    m ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}
The resulting 4 continuous angles Φ = (φ_0, φ_1, φ_2, φ_3) ∈ [-π, π]&sup4; are initialized onto the 4 data qubits via phase gates:
|ψ⟩ = ⨷_{m=0}³ R_z(φ_m) H |0⟩ = ⨷_{m=0}³ &frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (|0⟩ + e^{i φ_m} |1⟩)
